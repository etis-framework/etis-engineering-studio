# ETIS Engineering Studio Code of Conduct

## Purpose

ETIS Engineering Studio is intended to support serious engineering education and professional collaboration. Participation in the project should be respectful, evidence-oriented, inclusive, and safe for contributors with different backgrounds, institutions, levels of experience, and viewpoints.

## Expected behavior

Participants are expected to:

- treat other people with professional respect;
- critique ideas, designs, code, evidence, and decisions rather than attacking people;
- give specific, constructive feedback and receive it in good faith;
- acknowledge uncertainty and correct mistakes when new evidence warrants it;
- respect privacy, confidentiality, institutional policy, and intellectual property;
- avoid publishing student data, private repository contents, credentials, or personal information;
- make room for contributors with different communication styles and levels of experience;
- disclose meaningful conflicts of interest when they affect a project decision.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, intimidation, threats, stalking, or discriminatory abuse;
- sexualized or degrading language or imagery;
- sustained personal attacks, trolling, or deliberate disruption;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- knowingly exposing credentials, student-sensitive information, or private repository contents;
- retaliation against someone who raises a good-faith safety, security, or conduct concern.

## Scope

This code applies to repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, project communication, and situations in which someone is representing the ETIS Engineering Studio project. Institution-specific classroom conduct remains governed by the adopting institution's own policies as well.

## Reporting and enforcement

For routine moderation, contact the repository maintainers through the ETIS Framework organization or maintainer contact information. Do not use a public issue when reporting conduct that would expose private information or create additional harm.

Maintainers may edit or remove content, close or lock discussions, reject contributions, issue warnings, restrict participation, or ban participants when necessary to protect the project and its community. Decisions should be proportionate, documented privately when appropriate, and based on behavior rather than disagreement with an engineering position.

Security vulnerabilities should be reported under `SECURITY.md`, not through the conduct process.
